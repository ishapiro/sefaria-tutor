# How Shoresh Is Different

Shoresh is not another digital bookshelf. Apps like Sefaria excel at what they were built for: a vast library, layered commentaries (Rashi, Ramban, and many others), study calendars (Daf Yomi, 929, Parashat HaShavua), source sheets, and offline reading. Shoresh is an independent, free project that uses Sefaria only as a text source and focuses on something different: **foundation skills**—the grammar, roots, and vocabulary you need to read classical Jewish texts on your own.

Where many Torah and Hebrew apps rely on fixed flashcard decks, textbook word lists, or static interlines, Shoresh teaches **in context**. You read real passages from Tanakh, Talmud, liturgy, and more, tap any Hebrew or Aramaic phrase, and get a word-by-word breakdown with grammatical explanations: meaning, shoresh, binyan, gender, tense, part of speech, and notes—plus pronunciation at both phrase and word level. You are not memorizing isolated vocabulary; you are learning how biblical and rabbinic language works in the sentences where it actually appears.

That in-context learning feeds a full study loop. Save words to My Word List, review them with flashcards, attach private notes to verses, and use the Concordance Word Explorer to see how a root appears across texts and jump back into the reader. The About and Help pages put it simply: use Sefaria for breadth and traditional commentary; use Shoresh to build vocabulary and move toward independent reading—one word at a time, with AI-generated analysis (not Sefaria’s translations) as a bridge to the original sources.
